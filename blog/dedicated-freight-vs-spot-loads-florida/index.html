Dedicated Freight Lanes vs. Spot Loads: What Florida Shippers Should Know

When does it make sense to lock in a dedicated carrier instead of buying trucks load by load? A practical guide for logistics and procurement teams.

Call 24/7 (407) 595-9726Get a Quote

Every shipper eventually faces the same question: keep buying trucks on the spot market one load at a time, or commit to a carrier on dedicated or contract lanes? Both have a place. Here is how to think about it.

What "spot" really means

Spot freight is priced and booked load by load, usually close to the ship date. It's flexible — you only pay for what you move — but price and availability move with the market. When capacity is tight, rates jump and trucks get harder to find. You may also see a different carrier, and a different driver, on every load.

What "dedicated" or "contract" means

With dedicated or contract freight, you agree with a carrier on lanes, volume and a schedule for a period of time. The carrier plans its trucks and drivers around your freight. In return you get predictable capacity, consistent service and a rate that doesn't swing every week.

Signs you're ready for dedicated lanes

  • You ship the same origin and destination pairs every week
  • Missed or late pickups cause real problems on your dock or job site
  • You spend too much time re-booking trucks and chasing status
  • Your receivers expect the same appointment windows every time
  • You want one point of contact who already knows your freight

When spot still makes sense

Truly one-off shipments, unpredictable seasonal peaks and lanes you rarely use are good candidates for the spot market. Many companies run a hybrid: core recurring lanes on contract, overflow on spot.

Questions to ask a dedicated carrier

  • Are you an asset-based carrier or a broker?
  • How long have you been operating, and from where?
  • Which lanes do you already run regularly?
  • Who do I call for status, and are they reachable after hours?
  • What happens if a truck breaks down on my load?

That last question matters. At R&J Transport Service Inc we run heavy towing in-house, so a mechanical problem on a contract load doesn't turn into a search for a wrecker.

How to start

Pull a few months of shipment data, group it by lane and frequency, and share it with the carrier. A good carrier will come back with a rate and a realistic capacity commitment — not just a number. We have been running freight out of Orlando since 2004; send us your lanes and we'll build a plan.
